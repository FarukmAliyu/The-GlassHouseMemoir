Chapter Five

Who am I becoming? It is a simple question, but I have learned that simple questions can have complicated answers. I used to think that becoming someone meant achieving something visible like earning a degree, developing valuable skills, getting a good job, or accomplishing the goals I had set for myself. But life has gradually taught me that becoming is not only about what we achieve. It is also about what we learn, what we endure, what we believe, and the kind of person we choose to be along the way.When I look back at my journey, I see that I am not the same person who began it.Education changed me. Failure changed me. Faith changed me. Technology changed the way I see the world. Work taught me how to listen. Difficult moments taught me patience. Even the quiet moments, when I was alone with my thoughts, taught me things that no classroom could teach.I have learned that growth rarely announces itself.Sometimes it happens quietly.It happens when a person who once feared failure begins to see failure as a lesson. It happens when someone who constantly doubts themselves begins to recognize their own ability to continue. It happens when we stop expecting life to give us immediate answers and begin to understand the value of patience. My education has been an important part of this transformation. At Nigerian Turkish International College, I learned discipline, structure, and the importance of living alongside people with different backgrounds and perspectives. Later, studying Computer Science through the Distance Learning Centre at Ahmadu Bello University taught me something different: independence. Learning without always having classmates or lecturers physically around me required me to take responsibility for my own progress.The journey was not always easy. There were moments when I questioned whether I was capable enough. There were setbacks and times when I felt lost. But those experiences gradually changed my understanding of knowledge. I began to see education not simply as something that happens in classrooms, but as a lifelong process of learning from experience.Every challenge became another classroom.Programming taught me to approach problems piece by piece. When a program does not work, frustration alone does not solve it. You have to examine the problem, identify the error, make changes, and try again. In that sense, programming became more than a technical skill for me. It became a way of thinking about life.There are problems that cannot be solved immediately.There are situations where the answer is not obvious.There are moments when the first attempt fails.But failure does not necessarily mean the journey has ended. Sometimes it simply means that another approach is needed.Data analysis taught me another lesson: the importance of evidence. It is easy to make assumptions about the world, about people, and even about ourselves. But evidence can challenge assumptions. Learning to collect, organize, and interpret information has taught me to look more carefully before reaching conclusions.Artificial intelligence has expanded that curiosity even further. Technology is changing the world around us at an extraordinary pace. Sometimes I feel like a cyborg, half-human and half-machine, because technology has become so deeply connected to everyday life. Devices make work easier, information is available almost instantly, and artificial intelligence can perform tasks that once seemed impossible.Yet the more I learn about technology, the more I understand the importance of remaining human.A machine can process information, but information is not wisdom.An algorithm can recognize patterns, but recognizing a pattern is not the same as understanding a person's story.Technology can connect people across great distances, but connection is not always the same as companionship.The internet can provide answers, but not every important question can be answered by a search engine.I have had to remind myself of this.The digital world can be powerful, but it can also become distracting. It can influence what we believe, what we want, what we value, and even what we imagine our future should look like. I have learned that I must be careful not to allow technology to become my compass.The internet is a tool.It should help me think, not think for me.It should expand my possibilities, not define my identity.Some truths are found through information. Others are found through silence, reflection, faith, experience, and time.This is where faith continues to have a place in my life.Faith has taught me that I do not have to understand everything immediately. There are moments when the path ahead is unclear, and there are situations beyond my control. In those moments, prayer has given me a way to find peace and continue moving forward.My parents taught me that faith is not simply about asking for what I want. It is also about developing hope, gratitude, patience, and a sense of responsibility.I have come to understand that strength does not always look like certainty.Sometimes strength is continuing even when you are uncertain.Sometimes it is admitting that you do not know.Sometimes it is waiting.sometimes it is trusting that a difficult period does not have to define the rest of your life.
