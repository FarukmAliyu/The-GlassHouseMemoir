Chapter Six

My experience in sales taught me another important lesson about becoming,When Interned with School C Pilot Solutions, I initially thought sales was mostly about convincing people to buy something. I soon discovered that it was more about listening. People wanted to know how a product could solve their problems. They wanted someone to understand their needs rather than simply hear a prepared speech.That experience changed the way I communicate.Rejection also changed me.There were unanswered calls, closed doors, and conversations that went nowhere. At first, rejection felt personal. Eventually, I learned to separate rejection from identity. A person can hear “no” without becoming a failure.A closed door does not mean every door is closed. That lesson extends far beyond sales.Life itself contains rejection. Plans fail. Expectations change. Opportunities disappear. Sometimes we work hard for something and still do not get the result we expected. What matters is what we learn from the experience and whether we allow it to stop us from moving forward. I am also becoming more conscious of the world around me.When I think about the forests I remember from childhood, I understand that development cannot only be measured by buildings, technology, income, or economic growth. The environment is part of our future too.Trees, soil, water, wildlife, and the communities that depend on them are all connected. When forests disappear, something larger than trees is lost. Habitats disappear. Soil becomes vulnerable. Communities can suffer. The effects can extend far beyond the place where the trees were removed.This has made me think differently about progress. I want to be part of a future where development means creating opportunities without forgetting responsibility. I want knowledge to have a purpose.I want technology to solve problems rather than create new ones.I want skills to become opportunities not only for me, but for others.I believe Nigeria has enormous potential, particularly among its young people. Learning programming, data analysis, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and other computational skills can open new possibilities. But skills alone are not enough. They must be combined with integrity, creativity, discipline, empathy, and a willingness to contribute.That is the kind of person I hope to become.Not someone who knows everything, because I know that is impossible.Not someone who never fails, because failure is part of learning.Not someone who has every answer, because life will always contain questions.I want to become someone who continues learning.Someone who listens.Someone who thinks before accepting an answer.Someone who uses knowledge responsibly.Someone who remembers where he came from while remaining open to where he is going.When I think about the future, I do not see a perfectly planned road. I see possibilities.There will probably be challenges I cannot anticipate. There will be decisions I will get wrong. There will be moments when confidence disappears and moments when I surprise myself with how far I have come.But perhaps that is what becoming really means.It is not arriving at a final version of yourself.It is continuing to grow.The person I am today is the result of many people, experiences, lessons, failures, beliefs, and choices. My teachers helped shape me. My family gave me values. My education gave me knowledge. My work gave me experience. Faith gave me an anchor. Technology gave me new questions. Difficult moments gave me resilience.And I am still becoming.I do not know exactly where the journey will take me. But I know the kind of principles I want to carry with me: curiosity, patience, faith, integrity, compassion, resilience, and responsibility.Perhaps one day I will look back at the person writing these words and realize that I have changed again.And when that day comes, I hope I will still recognize the same desire that exists in me now—the desire to learn, to grow, to create something meaningful, and to leave the world a little better than I found it. For now, I am still on the journey still learning still questioning still becoming.
